# Research notes: copyright risk for a Polytopia-like game

## Key legal principles (US law)

**Idea–expression dichotomy.** Copyright Office: "Copyright does not protect the idea for a game, its name or title, or the method or methods for playing it... nothing in copyright law prevents others from developing another game based on similar principles." Game rules, mechanics, scoring systems, board grids, and win conditions are NOT protectable (fkks.com/Law360, Sean Kane 2017).

**What IS protected:** expressive elements — art style, visual design, characters, names/trademarks, music/sound, code, trade dress, and overall "look and feel."

**Tetris Holding v. Xio (D.N.J. 2012):** clone with identical look-and-feel (piece styles, colors, board dimensions, shadows, animations) = infringement even with rewritten code and new assets that mimic the original. Court: "if one has to squint to find distinctions... the works are likely substantially similar." Dr. Mario cited as the LEGAL way to build on Tetris ideas: same underlying concepts, unique original expression.

**Spry Fox v. 6Waves (Triple Town/Yeti Town, 2012):** pure reskin with identical mechanics + similar structure can still be risky; settled after surviving dismissal.

**DaVinci v. Ziko (Bang!, S.D. Tex. 2016):** identical rules but genuinely different theme, names, visual design → NO infringement. Rules "create the environment for expression" and are free.

**Trademark/trade dress:** names like "Polytopia", tribe names (Imperius, Bardur, Xin-Xi, Cymanti...), logo, and marketing likeness are protected separately from copyright. Do not use them.

## Practical guardrails for our game
1. Original name and branding ("Polyforge" — verify no conflict; do not mention Polytopia inside the game UI).
2. Do NOT reuse Polytopia tribe names, unit names where distinctive (Polytaur, Cymanti units), city naming style, or its exact visual assets. Generic terms (Warrior, Archer, Knight) are fine — scenes à faire in strategy genre.
3. Original art style: our low-poly Babylon 3D look with our own palette and geometry is distinct expression (Polytopia's flat pastel voxel-esque style is not copied; we render true 3D extruded tiles, different proportions, different color language).
4. Mechanics we keep (legal as ideas): turn-based 4X on a grid, star-like economy, tech tree, city leveling, unit stats/combat formulas, fog of war, 30-turn score mode.
5. Differentiate expression deliberately: different faction fantasy names, different tech names where distinctive, our own UI layout, our own map generation, our own logo/wordmark, plus gameplay improvements (asymmetric factions, late-game fixes) that make it genuinely a different game — like Dr. Mario vs Tetris.
6. Avoid marketing that references Polytopia or implies affiliation (no "Polytopia clone" in public-facing copy).
